How to interpret the experiment

Selection objective: minimize the training mean of (1 − live-sample rate) + live-sample rate × global TV; ties use mean traffic and then candidate ID. This combines availability with global uniformity. It does not optimize local sample diversity. “Tuned” means selected from the declared grid, not an exhaustive optimum.

Resource scope: memory is retained membership descriptors per node, including routing, reserve, history, and passive descriptors. Peaks are checked after each protocol callback, including bootstrap and the final drain. Transient RPC state, transaction snapshots, control buffers, and runtime overhead are excluded; these are not total RAM limits. Traffic is modeled compact-JSON bytes plus a fixed header, divided by live-node rounds after warmup. All maintenance, sampling, bootstrap, lost sends, and sends to dead targets are charged. The traffic ceiling is a per-run average, not an instantaneous link-rate cap.

Uniformity and resilience: global TV measures aggregate deviation from live-identity sampling; lower is better. Local TV excess compares empirical per-node sample diversity with a matched IID reference and is not a proof of marginal bias. Small negative excess values are possible from sampling variation. Final-quarter availability checks sustained churn. Recovery is the first observation after the crash with at least 95% in the largest strongly connected component, no more than 5% dead routing entries, and at least 95% successful samples. Recovery means and intervals include only recovered seeds; the recovered count is displayed alongside them.

Protocol scope: original PeerSwap is the unchanged simulator baseline, including its existing experimental join and failure handling. The repair extension adds bounded local recovery and reserve state; its novelty and theoretical guarantees have not been established. Shared membership, bootstrap, and initial-graph traces prevent protocol-specific workload advantages. Settings are frozen before evaluation; failed budget checks remain visible.

PeerSwap traffic model: this simulator uses a coordinator that sends full endpoint neighborhoods in prepare and apply messages, followed by acknowledgments. The authors’ pinned implementation exchanges neighborhoods between the two endpoints and sends small replacement messages to affected neighbors. Communication comparisons therefore apply to the simulator variants measured here, not directly to the authors’ implementation.

Uncertainty: confidence intervals describe seed-to-seed variation. Paired comparisons use matching evaluation seeds and workloads; their intervals are exploratory and have no multiple-comparison correction. Conditional recovery comparisons can involve fewer than ten paired observations.
